What the dots are: one neuron's path through the model, at one frequency k

Everything in this viewer is drawn for one frequency k at a time (one panel per frequency the model uses). At frequency k, a residue n is represented by a point on the unit circle, xk(n) = e−2πikn/43, a "clock hand" at angle −2πkn/43. Adding residues multiplies clock hands: xk(a)·xk(b) = xk(a+b). That is the whole trick the network has learned, and the quantities below say how each neuron takes part in it.

The quantities, in plain words

symbol (the buttons above drop the k; every neuron has one set per frequency)what it iswhat to look for
ak, bkThe neuron's two read coefficients: complex numbers saying how much of the frequency-k clock of token a (resp. token b) reaches the neuron's input, and with what phase shift. They come from the (k,0) and (0,k) entries of the 2-D Fourier transform of the neuron's pre-activation over all 43×43 inputs.A neuron that does this frequency's job has |a| ≈ |b| and both well away from zero. A neuron whose colour (dominant read frequency) differs from the panel's k has small a, b here.
ak·bkThe product of the two read coefficients. If the ReLU really multiplies the two clocks, the sum term it produces must have this phase.Compare with h: the two clocks in the detail table should point the same way.
hkThe sum term: the coefficient of xk(a+b) in the neuron's ReLU output (the (k,k) Fourier entry). It is the part of the neuron that knows a+b.arg h ≈ arg(a·b); |h| grows with |a|·|b|. This is the "coordinate-wise complex multiplication".
dkThe difference term: the coefficient of xk(a−b) (the (k,−k) entry). ReLU always makes it, with the same size as h.It is unavoidable per neuron; the model can only arrange for it to cancel in the sum. See d·w/2.
wkThe write coefficient: the frequency-k component of the neuron's weight vector into the logits (WoWU), i.e. the size and phase of the cosine wave it adds across the 43 answers.For the right answer to win, the write phase must undo the read phases: arg w ≈ −arg h.
κk = hk·wk/2The neuron's net contribution to the output clock Lk: the number multiplying xk(a+b) once the neuron's sum term is sent through its write vector.Should sit on the positive real axis. Then every neuron pushes the output clock the same way and the white arrow (Λk = Σκ) is long and real.
γk = hk·w̄k/2Leftover from pairing the sum term with the conjugate half of the write wave; it lands on a combination of a, b and the answer that is not a+b.Any phase, but the sum over neurons should be small compared with Λk.
dk·wk/2The difference term after the write: what the neuron adds that depends on a−b.Should cancel across neurons (small sum), otherwise a−b leaks into the answer.
ΛkThe total: how strongly the whole MLP outputs the clock xk(a+b) at frequency k, measured directly from the logits.Equals Σj κj if the picture is right; the stream check tab lists both.
dot colourThe neuron's dominant read frequency: the k at which its pre-activation has the most energy.In each panel the panel's own colour should dominate; other colours are neurons that mostly serve another frequency.

Plane, rings, phase map

PlaneThe literal picture: each dot is the chosen quantity as a point in the complex plane (distance from the centre = size, angle = phase). The white arrow is the sum over neurons. Good for seeing whether κ lines up on the positive real axis and how much each neuron contributes.
RingsOnly the phase is kept: every dot is placed on a circle at its angle, and the circle it sits on is its dominant read frequency (outer ring = the first frequency in the list). This is for when sizes differ so much that small dots pile up at the centre of the plane; it answers "do the phases agree?" without the magnitudes in the way.
Phase mapA scatter of read phase against write phase: x = arg(a·b), the phase the ReLU hands on, and y = arg w, the phase the neuron writes with. Since κ = h·w/2 and h ∝ a·b, a real positive κ means arg w = −arg(a·b), so every working neuron should lie on the dashed anti-diagonal y = −x. Dot size is √|κ|, so the neurons that matter are the big ones; a big dot off the line is a neuron that is fighting the others.
